# QA pass — fresh eyes, phone viewport (2026-09-26)

Two new customers, each with a throwaway account, taken from sign-in to account deletion on a phone-sized browser (390 × 844). We used the real API, worker and web app, and the real AI (Gemini free tier) on test mailboxes.

1. **Hearth & Wick Candles**: a small UK online shop, working in English. Its phone was set to en-GB and Europe/London.
2. **Nordwerk Digital GmbH**: a Berlin web agency (a services business). Its customers wrote in German, its phone was set to de-DE and Europe/Berlin, and the app itself was in English.

## How it was run

- **Setup:**
  - Local stack (`pnpm dev:stack --empty --gemini`), with the invite gate on (`SIGNUP_INVITE_CODES`, now passed through to the API).
  - Mailbox: a TLS mail server on the standard ports 993/465, connected through the normal "Other (IMAP/SMTP)" form. An operator then flagged it as a test mailbox, which is what lets the free AI tier process it.
  - Customer e-mails were real SMTP messages to that mailbox.
- **Not exercised end to end: e-mail sign-up and confirmation (Supabase Auth).**
  - The local stack signs in as a fixed development owner.
  - Opening the live sign-in page from this sandbox fails TLS: the sandbox's HTTPS proxy isn't trusted by the browser, and disabling TLS checks isn't allowed.
  - Instead we reviewed that flow in code (`apps/web/src/app/login/page.tsx`). The invite code, onboarding and everything after were used live.
- **Timing (free AI tier):**
  - A reply is drafted 2–40 s after the e-mail arrives.
  - A website of about 100 chunks takes a few minutes to read.
  - "Write with AI" takes about 4 s.
  - The free tier's daily quota for the quality model ran out mid-way through pass 2. We switched that model (`LLM_MODEL_QUALITY`) and continued.

## Needs your decision

- **D1. Sums the AI works out.** "Two classic candles = £36" was correct but blocked: the fact check only accepts numbers written in the knowledge. Options:
  - (a) keep it strict (safe, more hand-offs);
  - (b) allow totals the code can verify from knowledge prices (price × quantity, plus shipping);
  - (c) keep it strict but put the total into a draft for approval.

  We recommend (b), with the maths checked in code.

- **D2. UK bank details.** Invoices ask for IBAN and BIC. UK customers pay with sort code and account number. Should invoices get optional "sort code / account number" fields (shown for GB sellers)?
- **D3. App language.** The German agency used a fully English app (sign-in, onboarding, settings); only customer-facing text (replies, quotes, invoices, Accept page) is German. Should the app UI be localised, starting with German?
- **D4. Quote requests mixed with questions.** "What does a one-pager cost, is hosting included?" was treated as a quote request.
  - It didn't quote the one-pager (which is on the price list).
  - Because "hosting" isn't on the price list, it sent a clarifying question on its own in mode 2: "Could you tell us more about 'Hosting'?".
  - This follows the approved design (§21.3: anything unmapped → one clarifying question), but reads poorly.

  Proposal: quote what matches, and leave the rest to the normal reply (or to you). Alternatively, never send the clarifying question automatically.

- **D5. Running out of AI quota.** With the paid provider this should be rare. Should the owner (or you as admin) get an e-mail when e-mails are waiting because the AI provider's quota ran out, and should the app show it?
- **D6. Follow-ups on e-mails you start.** A new e-mail from Compose schedules a follow-up (for example "Follow-up 30 Sept") like a reply does. Keep that, or make it opt-in for e-mails you write yourself?
